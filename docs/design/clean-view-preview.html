Hide the Bars

A new button beside the on-screen keyboard switch hides the window's bars, with the device's name and the help line, so the device's screen fills the window up to its edges. Full screen still takes the whole monitor. Try both in the frames: they are the real windows, scaled down.

Another computer's window

The same button, beside Full screen, so both windows work alike.

  • Hide bars (the arrow pointing up, beside the keyboard switch) hides the top bar and the line at the bottom: the name and connection, the buttons, and the help and frame rate. The screen fills the window edge to edge, without the device's frame; what's left over is black, as in full screen.
  • A small bar stays at the top , as in full screen today, and fades after three seconds until the pointer comes near it: the keyboard switch, Show bars (the arrow pointing down) to bring everything back, sound, screenshot, and Full screen .
  • Full screen takes the whole monitor, from the main bar or the small one, and its button in the small bar then says Exit full screen . Leaving full screen goes back to where you were: the window without bars, or with them.
  • The computer viewer gets the same button, beside Full screen, so both windows work the same way; in a browser and in the tablet's app, Hide bars fills the page.
  • Each window opens with its bars; it doesn't remember that they were hidden.
