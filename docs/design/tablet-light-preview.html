The Tablet by Day

The Yes Machine app on Alejandro’s Tab A11+, at its real size. Press the sun beside Wi-Fi, at the top right of the app, as on the computer: Sarigüeya walks down to her house by day, and Android’s bars change with the app. Tap a computer to see signing in, and Connect to see the viewer.

  • The same switch as on the computer: the sun beside the Wi-Fi pill turns the app to day, and the moon back. On the home screen Sarigüeya climbs down from the night roof to the porch of her house by day while the light crosses it, as on the computer; on another page the mode just changes. The app remembers it.
  • The page scrolls to the house first. On the tablet the house sits below the This device card, so most of her walk would happen out of view; when part of the house is hidden, pressing the sun scrolls it into view and then she sets off. The computer has the same problem and could get the same.
  • Android’s bars change with the app: the status bar on top and the taskbar below turn light with dark icons by day, at the same moment the app’s colors do, and the app opens in the last mode chosen.
  • The house follows this device: by day the garage door and the front gates open while this device shares its screen (general mode). Sarigüeya sits up while a computer is ready to connect, as she does now at night, and wags her tail while a computer is in control.
  • The house’s bottom gets taller , as on the computer, so the day house’s fence, sidewalk and road fit; by night too, so nothing jumps when the mode changes.
  • Settings, dialogs and the This device card take the computer’s day colors. The rooftop cat page keeps her night picture, as on the computer.
  • On a phone the house is narrow: the porch and Sarigüeya fit, and the garage shows only at the right edge.
  • The viewer opens in the app’s mode: the white monitor and cream Sarigüeya by day, as in the computer’s viewer. Full screen stays black.
